For judges

Review submissions
without a spreadsheet.

You get a queue, not a folder. Each project opens with the submission, the repository link, the demo, the write-up and the rubric in one screen. Score, explain, submit, move on.

Sign in to start

Invited by an organizer? Open the link they emailed you. It works once, and only for the address it was sent to.

What the workspace gives you

  • The submission, the answers, the links and the tech stack on one screen
  • The rubric you must use, with weights and maximums from the organizer
  • A completeness checklist: what is missing from the entry, flagged server-side
  • Score ranges validated in the backend, not just in the form
  • Fields for what worked, what did not, and your recommendation
  • Save as a draft, submit when you are done, jump straight to the next one
  • Side-by-side comparison of your own scores on up to three projects
01

Your scores stay yours

A judge cannot read another judge’s scores — not through the interface, not through the API, not by changing a URL. The backend refuses it, and it refuses it for participants too.

02

You see only your assignments

Opening an unassigned project is a 403, not a hidden button. The assignment is what authorises the read, and it is checked on every request.

03

You are not alone

The organizer can see how far you have got without ever seeing what you wrote. If you finish early, nothing changes about your score weighting.

The loop

Open. Inspect. Score. Explain. Submit. Next.

Six steps, no navigation between pages. When you submit, the project is marked under review and the queue moves you to whatever is still open.

  1. 1
    Open

    The submission, its track, its team and its links.

  2. 2
    Inspect

    Repository, live demo, video, documentation, tech stack.

  3. 3
    Score

    Every criterion, with the range the organizer set.

  4. 4
    Explain

    What worked, what did not, and your recommendation.

  5. 5
    Submit

    Locked in, timestamped, and visible to the organizer.

  6. 6
    Next

    Straight into the next unfinished project.

A rubric looks like this

CriterionWeightMax
Does it work3510
Technical quality2510
Originality1510
Impact1510
Clarity1010

Weights are validated when the organizer saves them. A client cannot invent a criterion: an unknown criterion id is rejected with a 400.
